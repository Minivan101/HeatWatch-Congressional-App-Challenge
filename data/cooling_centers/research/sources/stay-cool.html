Stay Cool Clark County

Even though summers are getting hotter in Clark County, we want you to Stay Cool! Check out the useful tips and resources to help you turn down the heat.


Active Cooling Stations

Tips to Stay Cool at Home and More

Community Canopy Project

What Is a Cooling Station? Cooling stations are public, air-conditioned spaces that provide a safe place to cool off during extreme heat. They offer a comfortable, welcoming environment with water and hydration support to help protect community members from dangerous temperatures.

Why They Are Important: Clark County has experienced more than 40 days above 106°F in recent years, and the Urban Heat Island effect can make some neighborhoods significantly hotter than others. Extreme heat can affect anyone, making cooling stations an important resource for staying safe and preventing heat-related illness.

Stay cool this summer! Access the Stay Cool Clark County Resource Center for flyers, social media images, and other outreach materials to help spread the word about heat safety and available community resources.

“Where can I go with my dog to cool down?” “Which library is closest to me?” “Where’s the nearest splash pad?” “Is there a public pool nearby where I can take a dip to stay cool?”

With triple-digit weather already here, Our interactive, Stay Cool Clark County map is your best resource for up-to-date information on where you can find a little shelter from the sun.

How to Open the Map Menu

Hey! Trying to see the full list of spots to cool down on the map? Opening the side menu is super easy:

  • Look at the dark bar at the top of the embedded map.
  • Find the little square icon with an arrow pointing right, located in the top left corner next to the map's title.  
  • Just click that icon!
  • A sidebar will instantly slide out, showing you all the different categories you can check out, like cooling stations, splash pads, and libraries.

Stay cool out there!

Southern Nevada faces some of the most extreme heat in the country, with summer temperatures often rising above 100°F. This map showcases community facilities and resources that provide cool, safe places to escape the heat, along with splash pads and open pools. Select any icon on the map to see details about each location.

Free Bus Passes: Free 24-hour bus passes are available for individuals affected by extreme heat who need transportation to safely reach cooling stations and do not have other transportation options. Passes may be distributed at any time (not just during heat activations), with one pass available per visitor per visit; use the map to find participating libraries that distribute passes.

The Community Canopy Project

Put Down Roots in Your Neighborhood. Plant a Tree!

Put down roots in your community. Plant a tree!

We’re building an “urban forest” in Clark County to combat the urban heat island effect. And we’re doing it with your help. Since fall 2024, we’ve given away 4,500 trees annually to people in Clark County to plant trees on their property.

The trees will:

·         Create shade that will reduce air temperature by 12°F or more.

·         Potentially help people reduce home energy costs by shading HVAC units and windows.

·         Help clean the air by absorbing pollutants.


Registration is now open to ALL Clark County residents until the trees are all reserved.

Extreme Heat

Extreme heat days—days with temperatures exceeding 106° F—are projected to increase in Clark County. Currently, we experience about four extreme heat days per year. By 2064, that number could increase to 23 – 30 extreme heat days.

Staying cool in Clark County can be challenging, especially because not everyone experiences heat the same way. Due to the Urban Heat Island (UHI) effect, some of our neighborhoods are hotter than others. Although our extreme temperatures impact our vulnerable residents the most, everyone is exposed to extreme heat here in Clark County.

Download our Summer Heat Health Tips flier for more ways to Stay Cool!



Health and Well-Being Benefits of Trees in Desert Climates

  • Shade and Cooling: Reduce heat stress, lower temperatures, and provide comfortable outdoor spaces
  • Encouragement of Activity: Make walking and outdoor exercise more pleasant and safe.
  • Cleaner Air: Filter dust, pollutants, and improve overall air quality.
  • Mental Health: Green spaces reduce stress, promote relaxation, and enhance mood.
  • Food and Nutrition: Fruit-bearing trees provide fresh, local produce.
  • Community and Social Spaces: Shaded areas foster gatherings and social connections.
  • Biodiversity: Support birds and pollinators, enriching the local environment.
